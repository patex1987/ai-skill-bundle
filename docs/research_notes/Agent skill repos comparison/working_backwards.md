## Which published skills / repos implement Working Backwards, PR/FAQ, JTBD or customer-first discovery for coding agents, and which are curated or popular?

### Takeaway
No official Anthropic or Amazon Working Backwards skill exists (anthropics/skills, 188k stars, contains no PR/FAQ or Working Backwards file; Pocock's skills repo likewise). The credible options are third-party: BMAD's `bmad-prfaq` (inside a 55k-star, actively maintained repo, the best-engineered), `deanpeters/Product-Manager-Skills` `press-release` (7.9k stars, PM-oriented, press release only), and `amplitude/builder-skills` (163 stars, vendor-backed, single-prompt). Everything dedicated to Working Backwards by stars is obscure (0-26 stars). The closest thing to "customer-backwards" in the mainstream engineering skill sets is Pocock's `grilling` and `to-spec` (problem and solution "from the user's perspective", user stories), not a PR/FAQ.

### Inferences
- "Curated and widely used" holds for the host repos (BMAD, Product-Manager-Skills, lenny-skills), not for the Working Backwards skills themselves. The only dedicated Working Backwards skill with real traction-adjacent backing is `bmad-prfaq` (via BMAD's 55k stars), but it is coupled to BMAD's `_bmad/` machinery and so would need to be reimplemented, not installed, for a generic catalog.
- Amplitude's single prompt and robdefeo's two-phase skill are the easiest to adapt (plain markdown, no runtime). siddarthnyati's traceability and provenance-tag ideas are the transferable pieces for a planner handoff; its whole pipeline is too heavy for an engineering-planning catalog.
- Pocock's `to-spec` is effectively a "light working backwards" already (user-perspective problem + solution + user stories) so a catalog skill should be framed as complementary to a spec/plan step, not a competitor.

## What is the canonical Working Backwards artifact structure (press release, FAQs, tenets, "five questions"), citing Amazon / Bryar / Carr sources?

### Takeaway
Canonical artifact: a press release under one page (heading, subheading, summary paragraph, problem, solution, spokesperson quote, customer quote, how to get started) plus an external FAQ and an internal FAQ, FAQ at most five pages, written from the customer's side first. The "five customer questions" (customer, problem/opportunity, most important benefit, how do you know what they need, experience) are widely attributed to Bryar and Carr's book, but I could not retrieve the book's own wording; Amazon's own page and workingbackwards.com list a different set (seven reviewer questions) and do not use the "five questions" label.

### Cited Findings
- Amazon (aboutamazon.com, text by the authors): the process "begins by defining the customer experience" and moves backward until the team knows what to build; "a systematic way to vet ideas and create new products"; main tool is the PR/FAQ; press release is "always less than one page"; FAQ "should be five pages or less"; "there are no awards for extra pages or more words"; teams often write ten or more drafts and meet senior leaders five or more times; most PR/FAQs never become products, "a feature, not a bug"; the document is "a living document" — [An insider look at Amazon's culture and processes](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/an-insider-look-at-amazons-culture-and-processes) (fetched 2026-11-07; the fetch summary noted the page does not mention "the five questions")
- Press release components per workingbackwards.com (Bryar and Carr's site): Heading (name + one sentence), Subheading (target customer and benefit), Summary paragraph (city, media outlet, launch date plus summary), Problem paragraph (customer's POV), Solution paragraph(s) (how it solves and differs from alternatives), Quotes and Getting Started (one spokesperson quote, one hypothetical customer quote, how to start) — [workingbackwards.com resource page](https://www.workingbackwards.com/resources/working-backwards-pr-faq/) and [concepts page](https://www.workingbackwards.com/concepts/working-backwards-pr-faq-process/) (fetched 2026-11-07)
- FAQ: external FAQs answer plain-language press/customer questions (price, how it works, support, where to buy); internal FAQs anticipate senior leaders and every department (finance, marketing, support, operations, HR): current alternatives, problem size/TAM/willingness to pay, per-unit economics and upfront investment, technical/legal/regulatory/third-party dependencies, assumptions that must hold, "top three reasons the product could fail". "An excellent FAQ demonstrates mastery of every aspect of the product." — [workingbackwards.com concepts page](https://www.workingbackwards.com/concepts/working-backwards-pr-faq-process/)
- Reviewer criteria on workingbackwards.com are seven: customer clearly defined; problem clearly defined; does the solution address the problem; would customers change behaviour to adopt; better/cheaper/faster than substitutes; TAM and payback big enough; what constraints must be solved — [concepts page](https://www.workingbackwards.com/concepts/working-backwards-pr-faq-process/)
- Tenet: Amazon's first Leadership Principle, Customer Obsession: "Leaders start with the customer and work backwards" — [workingbackwards.com resource page](https://www.workingbackwards.com/resources/working-backwards-pr-faq/)
- Process rhythm: first draft "a few hours, not a few days"; review meeting starts with 15-20 min silent reading then ~40 min discussion; most senior attendees speak last; go/no-go before the Agile build phase; no-go reasons include copycat, small TAM, risky investment, infeasible today, backlog priority — [concepts page](https://www.workingbackwards.com/concepts/working-backwards-pr-faq-process/), [Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/an-insider-look-at-amazons-culture-and-processes)
- "Five questions" list as used by the skills: who is the customer; what is the customer problem or opportunity; what is the most important customer benefit; how do you know what customers need or want; what does the customer experience look like. A web-search summary reported this list from several secondary sources and flagged that none quotes the book — [search result digest](https://nidhiwadmark.substack.com/p/working-backwards); the list is reproduced as the skill's own framework in [robdefeo five-questions-guide.md](https://github.com/robdefeo/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/amazon-working-backwards/references/five-questions-guide.md)
- Bryar and Carr themselves are described (in a PM skill's README, secondary) as saying the PR/FAQ ends in a go/no-go and Agile picks up afterwards — [siddarthnyati README](https://github.com/siddarthnyati/working-backwards/blob/main/README.md) (secondary; unverified against the book)

### Inferences
- The two Amazon-authored pages do not reproduce the "five questions" as a labelled list; the seven reviewer questions on workingbackwards.com cover the same ground (customer, problem, solution fit, behaviour change, differentiation). For a catalog skill, attribute the five questions to "Working Backwards (Bryar and Carr, 2021)" cautiously, and describe them as the commonly cited customer questions rather than quoting.
- Hard length limits (PR under one page, FAQ at most five pages) are Amazon's own and are the main lever for making an agent version lightweight.

### Gaps
- No direct text from the book *Working Backwards* (2021) or Amazon "tenets" documents beyond the Customer Obsession principle; the term "tenets" in the brief was not found as a distinct PR/FAQ component on the pages read.
- AWS blog posts and the Bryar interviews (e.g. antoinebuteau.com lessons, productmasterynow episode 448) were found in search but the fetch failed (usage limit), so no first-hand statement from Bryar on lighter-weight use.

## When does PR/FAQ help for engineering tasks vs be ceremony, and is there a lightweight version suited to agent planning?

### Takeaway
PR/FAQ is a go/no-go and clarity device for new customer-facing value, not a design or refactor tool; the skills themselves say so (Product-Manager-Skills: not for trivial features; siddarthnyati: don't run Full mode on everything; Amazon: used at the start, before building). A lightweight form already exists in the wild in three shapes: the "5 Questions" phase on its own (robdefeo), a one-page brief (AI-DLC "Workshop Vision"/"PRF"), and Pocock/BMAD-style interviews that end in a user-perspective problem + solution + stories.

### Cited Findings
- Amazon positions the PR/FAQ "at the beginning of the new product development process", before Agile build, as a funnel for vetting many ideas, with a first draft in hours — [workingbackwards.com concepts](https://www.workingbackwards.com/concepts/working-backwards-pr-faq-process/)
- Product-Manager-Skills says use for "a new product or major feature", not for "trivial features (don't over-engineer small tweaks)" — [press-release/SKILL.md](https://github.com/deanpeters/Product-Manager-Skills/blob/main/skills/press-release/SKILL.md)
- siddarthnyati: modes Full / Targeted / Lightweight; Lightweight (intake, press release, internal FAQ, requirements) "mandate or compliance work — the what is fixed"; "A compliance mandate with a fixed deadline doesn't need a demo or a telemetry plan. Heavy process applied to light problems is why methods get abandoned." — [SKILL.md](https://github.com/siddarthnyati/working-backwards/blob/main/plugins/working-backwards/skills/working-backwards/SKILL.md)
- The same skill's README argues the real risk of a solo-user-plus-model PR/FAQ: it "replaces the document and not the room", producing a structured artifact chain "resting on invented numbers"; its mitigations are provenance tags, no invented figures, and blockers as questions for named humans — [README.md](https://github.com/siddarthnyati/working-backwards/blob/main/README.md)
- BMAD adapts the method to non-commercial concepts (internal tool, open source) by swapping unit economics/customer acquisition for maintenance burden/adoption path — [internal-faq.md](https://github.com/bmad-code-org/BMAD-METHOD/blob/main/skills/bmad-prfaq/references/internal-faq.md)
- Amplitude's version adds a "kill test" and mandates at least one unanswered internal FAQ — honesty features that suit an agent that tends to over-agree — [amazon-working-backwards/SKILL.md](https://github.com/amplitude/builder-skills/blob/main/product-skills/skills/amazon-working-backwards/SKILL.md)
- Light shapes that already exist: robdefeo's 5Q-only path (writes `YYYY-MM-DD - X 5Q.md` without the PR-FAQ) — [SKILL.md](https://github.com/robdefeo/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/amazon-working-backwards/SKILL.md); AI-DLC "Workshop Vision" and "PRF" one-pagers — [mavimariasme SKILL.md](https://github.com/mavimariasme/working-backwards-skill/blob/main/skills/working-backwards/SKILL.md); Superpowers brainstorming sizes the output ("small change gets a short design doc") — [superpowers.md](./superpowers.md)

### Inferences (my judgement, not sourced)
- Helps: new user-facing features, new platform capabilities whose consumers are other teams/services (the "customer" is the API consumer or developer), anything where the real problem is unclear or a solution is being proposed as a problem ("we need X"). In the movie platform: a new recommendation capability, an agent/MCP tool surface, a booking-flow change.
- Ceremony: refactors, dependency upgrades, CDK/infra changes with no new consumer-visible behaviour, bug fixes with clear repro, mechanical migrations. For these the planner's existing "restate goal, who benefits, what stays unchanged" is enough.
- Risk: invented customer quotes/metrics (mitigate: label as illustrative, `[NEEDS EVIDENCE]`), marketing-speak bloat (mitigate: size caps), gate fatigue on small work (mitigate: trigger on classification and light mode), and the brief becoming a second source of truth that drifts from the plan (mitigate: planner links to it, does not copy it).
- Fastest-value subset for an agent: the 5 Questions (especially "how do we know" and "what does the experience look like") plus a short internal FAQ whose answers become requirements, unknowns and acceptance-test seeds; the narrative press release is the high-cost part and could be optional (full mode).

### Gaps
- No independent critique (blog posts, Bryar interviews) of PR/FAQ for engineering/refactor work was retrieved; the usage limit hit before the "when not to use" search. The "ceremony for refactors/infra" position rests on the skills' own anti-pattern text and inference.
- No empirical evidence that a PR/FAQ-first step improves agent plan quality; the only evals found are the author's own (siddarthnyati `evals/`, unverified here) and Superpowers' release-note numbers about brainstorming behaviour, not PR/FAQ.

## Design options for a catalog skill and how the planner (principal-engineer-planner) would consume it

### Takeaway
Recommend Option A: a small, optional "customer-brief" skill (light = Five Questions + 5-8 line FAQ; full = adds a one-page press release and external/internal FAQ), writing `docs/briefs/<feature>.md`, with the planner reading it as the source for goal, requirements, unknowns and acceptance-test seeds when it exists. Options B (extend the planner's step 2 inline) and C (BMAD-style full PR/FAQ skill) are alternatives.

### Context on the current planner
`catalog/skills/principal-engineer-planner/SKILL.md` (read 2026-11-07, local repo) starts: classify task, restate the goal (what changes, who benefits, what stays unchanged, assumptions, non-goals), inspect context, identify unknowns, explore alternatives, then requirements, agreed acceptance tests and steps; it has light/standard/design-review depth. A brief fits before step 2 and fills "who benefits" and unknowns with evidence.

### Options (my recommendations, built from the sourced material above)

**Option A (recommended): `customer-brief` (working-backwards-lite) as a separate, opt-in skill**
- Name: `working-backwards` or `customer-brief` (description should say it is the pre-plan discovery step; keep catalog content generic, per the repo's portability lint).
- Trigger: user-facing feature, new platform capability or API/MCP surface, or a vague "we should build X"; planner classification "New feature" with depth Standard/Design review and an unclear customer or problem. Explicitly skip: refactor, bug with repro, infra/dependency change, mechanical migration. Planner offers it, never forces it.
- Modes: Light (default): Five Questions (customer, problem, most important benefit, how we know (evidence tagged observed/reported/assumed/unknown), experience as a short happy-path walkthrough) + "what stays unchanged / not for" + 5-8 hostile internal-FAQ questions with honest answers, at least one explicitly unanswered, capped around one page. Full: adds the press release (heading, subheading, problem, solution, spokesperson and customer quote labelled illustrative, getting started), external FAQ, internal FAQ, and a verdict (go / rework / kill, "forged / needs heat / cracks" à la BMAD).
- Output: `docs/briefs/<feature>.md` (frontmatter: status, mode, evidence-quality, linked plan; sections numbered so the plan can cite them, e.g. `Brief: 5Q-3`, `IFAQ-2`). Optionally a distillate block "Requirement signals / open questions / scope in-out" (BMAD's distillate idea) at the bottom rather than a second file.
- Planner consumption: step 2 "Restate the Goal" cites the brief's customer + benefit instead of restating from scratch; step 3 treats brief evidence tags as unknowns to resolve; step 4 imports unanswered internal-FAQ items as questions; requirements carry `Source: brief 5Q-n/IFAQ-n` (siddarthnyati traceability) and requirements without a source are flagged as scope additions; the "Experience" section seeds the acceptance-test proposals the user then agrees (acceptance tests remain agreed with the user, not auto-derived); the plan links the brief, does not copy it.
- Key sections: Customer (primary/secondary, who is NOT served), Problem (current workaround, cost, why now), Benefit (one), Evidence ledger (claim, tag, source), Experience walkthrough (happy path + one failure path), Not-doing, Internal FAQ (feasibility, dependencies, top 3 failure reasons, unanswered), Verdict/next step.
- Guards: no invented figures (`[NEEDS EVIDENCE]`), customer quotes labelled illustrative, questions for humans rather than findings, short caps.
- Cost: one more artifact to maintain; risk of ceremony if over-triggered.

**Option B: no new skill, add a "Customer lens" substep to the planner**
- Change planner step 2 for user-facing work only: answer the Five Questions in 5-8 lines inside the plan's "Goal" section, using Pocock-style recommended answers for the user to accept ("➡️ recommended"), plus an "evidence" tag on each. Output stays inside the plan file, no `docs/briefs/`.
- Pros: zero new artifacts, smallest diff, matches the repo's "reuse existing" ethos and Pocock's rule that composable behaviour does not get a new skill. Cons: no reusable artifact for non-planner uses (stakeholder alignment), planner grows, the press-release thinking is lost.

**Option C: full PR/FAQ skill modelled on `bmad-prfaq` (interactive coach + verdict + distillate)**
- Trigger: explicit only ("run the PR/FAQ challenge") for new product capabilities or platform bets; headless mode generating a first draft from supplied notes. Output `docs/briefs/<feature>/prfaq.md` and `.../distillate.md`; the planner consumes only the distillate (requirement signals, scope in/out, open questions) so context stays small.
- Pros: strongest challenge to weak ideas; proven stage structure. Cons: heavy for engineering tasks, subagent fan-out (artifact analyzer, web researcher) duplicates `codebase-research`/`external-research`; best treated as a "full" mode of Option A rather than a standalone default.
